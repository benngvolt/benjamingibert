<p><em>Nightingales</em> is a <em>musical fiction</em> in four tracks, a secret rave born in the heart of the trees, a place where chimeras and streams improvise together in a shared language.</p><br>

<p>It is an imaginary ecosystem made of <em>concrete textures</em>, <em>layers</em>, <em>rhythms</em> and <em>harmonic intertwinings</em>, a space in which one dives to dance. I cultivate a fascination for everything that happens naturally, for what is accidental and escapes me—whether in the environment or in my own mind.</p><br>

<p>The impulse was born from a walk in the forest, on the morning after a house festival. In the contrast between the echoes of a festive night and the morning calm, a question arose: <em>what if I invented my own sanctuary for dance?</em> My favorite activity: venturing into immersion, always on the edge between introspection and excitement for external phenomena.</p><br>

<p>Here appear fragments inherited from the electronic scene of the 90s and 2000s: patterns borrowed from <em>house</em>, <em>IDM</em>, <em>UK garage</em>, ending in a <em>futuristic vocal polyphony</em> before sunrise.</p><br>

<p>A distinctive feature of the EP is the gradual emergence of the <em>human voice</em>, without words or language, in the form of <em>phonemes</em>, <em>breaths</em> and <em>improvised fragments</em>. Used as <em>raw material</em>, it gradually seeps into the tracks, not aiming to signify, but simply to resonate with the other elements.</p><br>

<p>The <em>nightingale</em>, emblematic bird of the title, is known for its nocturnal song. Here, it becomes the symbol of a <em>liminal space</em>: between wakefulness and sleep, between nature and synthesis.</p>
